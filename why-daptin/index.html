A backend architecture decision

Choose one backend.
Not seven islands.

Daptin is for products whose data, identity, files, automation, integrations, realtime behavior, and business controls need to agree. It trades mandatory managed infrastructure for a compact runtime you operate and own.

THE CENTRAL DIFFERENCE

Integration is the architecture,
not an afterthought.

Many backend stacks assemble separate products for data, identity, jobs, files, events, integrations, and metering. Daptin places those concerns around one resource graph and one permission model.

01

One model

Schema, relationships, validation, metadata, permissions, and events start from the same resource definition.

02

One policy boundary

Users and groups govern rows, relations, actions, topics, credentials, and metered operations.

03

Many standard surfaces

JSON:API, GraphQL, OpenAPI, WebSockets, OAuth/OIDC, OpenAI-compatible routes, files, and optional protocols.

04

One deployable runtime

A Go binary starts compact and can coordinate shared database, storage, cache, PubSub, and counters as demand grows.

DECISION FRAMEWORK

Choose for the constraint
that matters most.

No backend is right for every team. The useful question is which responsibility you want to own and which kind of complexity your product actually has.

CHOOSE DAPTIN WHEN

Connected capability and ownership matter.

  • You want APIs, auth, files, jobs, integrations, realtime, or metering to share one policy model.
  • You need self-hosting, standard databases, portable schemas, and source-visible behavior.
  • Your product benefits from unusual protocol breadth or an OpenAI-compatible gateway.
  • A single operational runtime is easier for your team than stitching and securing several services.
CONSIDER A MANAGED BAAS WHEN

Operational delegation is the priority.

  • You want a vendor to operate the database, global delivery, scaling, and most service reliability.
  • Your application maps cleanly to that provider’s services and commercial model.
  • Cloud-specific SDKs, control planes, or deployment constraints are acceptable.
  • Your team values managed infrastructure more than runtime portability.
BUILD CUSTOM SERVICES WHEN

Your domain logic is the platform.

  • The backend is dominated by specialized computation, unusual consistency, or custom network behavior.
  • Independent service scaling and ownership are requirements, not future possibilities.
  • Your team can maintain identity, policy, observability, deployment, and integration glue deliberately.
  • Generated resource APIs would be incidental rather than central to the product.

HONEST BOUNDARIES

What Daptin does not pretend to be.

Not a managed cloud

Daptin provides the application runtime. You remain responsible for production infrastructure, backups, monitoring, upgrades, and incidents.

Not an unlimited workflow engine

Actions, schedules, and state tracking cover backend orchestration; they do not claim full BPM semantics or every distributed workflow pattern.

Not every protocol in full

Some optional surfaces—especially CalDAV/CardDAV—are deliberately basic and should be evaluated against the exact compatibility you need.

Not a substitute for domain design

A generated API cannot decide your resource boundaries, security model, failure behavior, or product rules for you.

VERIFY THE CLAIMS

The difficult surfaces have runnable demos.

Inspect focused repositories for LLM API compatibility, OAuth/OIDC provider flows, per-user integration credentials, and metered credit accounting.
