THE PRACTICAL DECISION

Daptin, a managed backend,
a CMS, or custom code.

There is no universal winner. Choose based on the product you are building, the experience your team needs, and who should operate the backend.

CHOOSE DAPTIN WHEN

You need a broad backend on your infrastructure.

  • The product needs several of these together: data APIs, customer accounts, row permissions, uploads, scheduled operations, integrations, realtime updates, or usage limits.
  • You want to keep application data and credentials in infrastructure you control.
  • A portable server and standard HTTP APIs matter more than a vendor-specific cloud SDK.
  • Your team is prepared to operate the server, database, storage, backups, and monitoring.
CHOOSE A MANAGED BACKEND WHEN

You want somebody else to operate it.

  • Firebase, Supabase Cloud, Appwrite Cloud, or Convex already cover the product’s needs.
  • Managed database operations, scaling, delivery, and reliability are worth the ongoing service cost.
  • The provider’s SDKs, region choices, limits, and product roadmap are acceptable.
  • Your team would rather build product features than run backend infrastructure.
CHOOSE CMS OR CUSTOM CODE WHEN

Editing or specialized computation is the core job.

  • Choose Directus, Strapi, Payload, Keystone, or another CMS when editorial workflows and content authoring are the center of the product.
  • Build custom services for specialized computation, strict latency requirements, or independently scaled workloads.
  • Custom code is also the better fit when the domain cannot be expressed clearly as records plus server-side operations.
  • Your team accepts the cost of maintaining its own auth, policy, deployment, and integration code.

CURRENT LIMITS

Check these limits
before choosing Daptin.

You do not want to run production

Daptin is self-hosted. Your team is responsible for backups, monitoring, upgrades, capacity, security, and incidents.

You need a full workflow engine

Actions can run ordered steps, schedules can repeat work, and status rules can block invalid changes. Use a dedicated workflow product for parallel branches, nested processes, and business-process modeling.

A protocol must work with a specific client

Optional mail, FTP, CalDAV, and CardDAV services cover particular use cases. Test the exact client and behavior you need before depending on them.

Your backend is mostly specialized computation

Media processing, simulation, hard realtime work, and unusual transaction models are usually clearer as custom services.
