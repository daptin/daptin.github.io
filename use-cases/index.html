Complete product stories

See the experience—not a bag of backend features.

A useful product journey crosses many boundaries: a person joins, gains access, creates work, adds content, triggers a decision, hears back, and returns later. These examples show how Daptin keeps that whole journey connected.

02 · Connected-service products

Let customers bring the tools they already use.

A connected account should unlock a useful experience, not expose a wall of provider mechanics.

A customer chooses an outside service, grants access on that service’s own consent screen, and returns to your product. Daptin can remember which credential belongs to that customer and make supported provider operations available to a product workflow.

Your interface can then say “import these contacts,” “publish this item,” or “sync this folder.” The provider call stays behind a named, permissioned action; the result can update a record, produce a file, send a message, or notify the customer.

This keeps provider secrets away from the browser and lets product language remain consistent even when several systems take part.

04 · Mail-enabled workflows

Make email part of the product record.

Useful when customers already send requests, documents, replies, or approvals through email.

An incoming message can become application data instead of disappearing into a separate shared inbox. Relate it to a customer, case, order, or project; preserve attachments; and let the team handle it through the same access rules as the rest of the product.

Actions and templates can prepare a response or notification. Outbound work can be queued and retried, keeping delivery attempts visible when something goes wrong.

Customers may never know there is a mail server involved. They experience a product that remembers the conversation and connects it to the work.

Support intake

Turn a message into a case, keep attachments, assign it, and send status updates.
